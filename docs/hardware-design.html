Recorder hardware options

Block diagrams and parts for the outdoor recorder. Every block with a dashed amber border and a C# tag is a decision with more than one reasonable answer; the tables under each diagram compare them. Power figures are rough estimates for sizing, not measurements. Measure the prototype with a USB power meter before buying solar.

What's fixed already (from docs/hardware.md): the recorder only records and uploads; BirdNET runs indoors on pipeline/analyzer.py, which picks up finished audio files named by UTC start time (e.g. 20260512T131500Z.flac). Seattle winters set the power budget. Recording daylight only (plus optionally an hour after dusk for owls) is roughly half the energy of 24 hours. A cable run to the house is probably possible but not preferred, so the plan is solar, with the cable as the fallback. Default schedule: continuous through daylight, with the schedule configurable (below), so size the solar for continuous recording.

Not modeled yet, and the biggest remaining savings: recording on a schedule (next section). On the ESP32-S3, CPU idling plus recording 1 minute in 3 outside the dawn chorus would take a winter day from ~1.4 to roughly 0.5 Wh.

What that means

  • On the ESP32-S3, keeping the processor awake is ~60% of the budget. The radio is only ~20%. The I²S peripheral needs clocks running, so the chip can't light-sleep while recording. The levers are lowering the CPU clock (40 MHz may be enough for one channel of FLAC), using one core, and letting DMA fill large buffers so the CPU idles between them. That could plausibly cut a third, but it has to be measured.
  • On the nRF5340 the processor sleeps between buffers, so upload becomes the biggest item with FLAC, and encoding becomes the biggest with Opus. Switching to Opus moves energy from the radio to the CPU: a modest net win (~25%), not the 5× the file size suggests.
  • Small always-on drains matter at this scale. A dev board's power LED (1–2 mA all day) would be ~0.1–0.16 Wh, about 10% of build A: desolder it or cut its trace. microSD cards can idle at ~0.5 mA, so switch the card's power off at night.
  • The microphone is never the problem (1–9%). Choose it for sound quality, not power.
  • Stereo costs ~30% more on the ESP32-S3, almost all of it from uploading twice the data.
  • The Pi's baseline is the issue: 0.45 W just to be on, before recording anything. The night timer saves more than everything else combined.
  • Not included above: charging losses. A linear charger like the BQ24074 is ~70–80% efficient from panel to battery, and the panel rarely sits at its best voltage. That affects how big a panel to buy, not the daily consumption: add ~30–40% when sizing panels.

Recording schedule

Recording all day, continuously, is the most expensive part of every build. Long-term acoustic monitors usually record on a schedule instead. The trade-off is energy against short or rare calls.

ScheduleEnergy (ESP32-S3, winter)ProsCons
Continuous, dawn to dusk (the default)~1.0–1.4 WhHears everything, including one-off calls and fly-overs; counts need no correctionBiggest panel and battery
Sliced: 1 min on, 2 min off, all day~0.4–0.5 WhAbout a third of the recording energy and data; birds that sing repeatedly are still caught most daysMisses some short, single calls (fly-overs, an alarm call); minutes heard are a sample and need scaling (×3) or labelling; the timeline shows gaps
Continuous for ~3 h around dawn, sliced the rest of the day~0.6–0.7 WhKeeps the dawn chorus whole, when most species sing; most of the savings of slicingSlightly more complex firmware and dashboard ("sampled" vs "continuous" hours)
Wake on sound (mic with a loudness trigger, e.g. Vesper VM3011)Lowest when quietSleeps through silenceDistant or quiet birds may never trip the threshold; wind and rain trip it constantly; least predictable

Configurable: the schedule lives in the same SD-card config file as the mic profile, so it can change without re-flashing. Suggested settings: start and end relative to sunrise and sunset (e.g. −60 min / +30 min, or +90 min for owls), an optional on/off slice length (e.g. 1 / 2 min, off by default), an optional continuous window around dawn when slicing, and a low-battery rule (e.g. switch to slices below 40% charge, stop below 15%) so a gray week degrades the data instead of killing the recorder.

The analyzer and dashboard need to know which minutes were recorded: each file's length is already in its name and duration, and "minutes heard" would be shown out of "minutes recorded".

Swapping microphones

SwapWiringFirmware
Same type, e.g. I²S MEMS ↔ I²S MEMS (ICS-43434, SPH0645, INMP441)Same 5 wires: 3.3 V, ground, bit clock, word clock, data (+ L/R select)Small config change: bit depth and timing quirks (the SPH0645 has one)
I²S ↔ PDMDifferent pins: PDM uses clock + data onlyDifferent peripheral; trivial if the firmware supports both from the start (ESP32-S3, nRF5340 and nRF52840 all have both)
Digital ↔ analog EM272Add a codec board (e.g. WM8960) between mic and MCUCodec setup once; after that the MCU sees I²S, so everything downstream is unchanged
  • Mic pods on a connector. Each mic lives in a small housing on a short cable with an M8 or M12 connector (5–8 pins) through the enclosure wall, with one pinout for every pod: 3.3 V, ground, both clocks, data, L/R select. Swapping is unplugging a pod, not opening the sealed box. Keep digital mic cables under ~30–50 cm, twisted or shielded; analog EM272 cables can be much longer.
  • Mic profiles in firmware, chosen in the SD-card config file (interface, bit depth, gain), so no re-flashing.
  • Record which mic made each recording. Mics differ in sensitivity and noise, so BirdNET's confidence shifts after a swap. A mic label in each file's metadata, and later a marker on the dashboard, keeps a mic change from looking like a change in the birds.

A. Low-power MCU, one mic

B. Low-power MCU, two mics (stereo)

Everything from A stays the same except the microphone stage and the data rate. Two identical digital mics share one bus: I²S and PDM both carry a left and a right channel on the same clock, so the two channels are sampled in lockstep, which is exactly what direction-finding needs. See "Counting individual birds" in docs/hardware.md.

If the mic is analog (EM272) instead: stereo needs a stereo codec (C2, e.g. WM8960) between the mics and the MCU, and the two capsules should be a matched pair. On the Pi (build C) it needs a stereo-input sound card; most cheap USB dongles are mono.

Suggested path

  1. Bench prototype of A on USB power: ESP32-S3 + one ICS-43434 + microSD, writing 5-minute 32 kHz FLAC files named by UTC start time, uploading to a small receiver on the indoor computer. Measure real power with a USB meter, and compare BirdNET results against a phone recording side by side.
  2. Add a second mic (build B) on the bench; check both channels line up. Cheap, and it tells you whether direction-finding is worth the bar outdoors.
  3. Size solar from the measured numbers, then add charger, 18650 and panel. Test the two 4.8 V cells as a summer-only supply at the same time.
  4. Keep C as the fallback if MEMS mics miss too many quiet birds, or if a cable run to the house turns out to be possible.
